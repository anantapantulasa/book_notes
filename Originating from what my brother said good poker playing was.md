Similarly, can we do the same in our thinking? Not necessarily by thinking about how others view our life, but in ways we can game our lives to getting +EV results. Essentially, it got me thinking about the different orders of thinking.

First-order thinking can be done by just about anyone. That doesn't mean its a bad thing, it's something to consider when attempting to go against the grain. First-order thinking is about solving the immediate problem with an immediate decision. This is usually associated with instant satisfaction and not necessarily a long-term solution.

This is where second-order thinking comes into play. After considering the first-order thinking, one could ask themselves, "and then what?" We can consider the first-order decision over time and other variables. 

Let's say you make a business decision to cut marketing for a specific product. First-order thinking might be satiated with a decrease in costs, and thus an increase in profit. Potential second-order thinking considers (1) employees and the future costs of removing or keeping them, (2) competitor response, (3) demand flow, (4) in-house inventory affects and potential future value in new projects.
